# Q4.3 Constrained block: stress to restore the y dimension, then the volume change

> **Source**: Lambda Feedback · MECH40005 Stress Analysis 1 (2026–27) · Tutorial sheet 4: Stress and strain · Problem 4.3 (two parts)
> **Keywords**: 3D Hooke's law, thermal strain, superposition, constraint $\varepsilon_y=0$, volumetric strain

## 1. Problem

> A steel ($E=207\ \text{GPa}$, $\nu=0.3$, $\alpha=11\times10^{-6}\ \text{K}^{-1}$) block with rectangular faces has the dimensions 50 mm, 75 mm and 100 mm in the directions of the three coordinate axes $x$, $y$ and $z$ respectively.
>
> **(a)** If the block is subjected to tensile stresses of 20 MPa and 60 MPa in the $x$ and $z$ directions respectively and its temperature is raised by 20 °C, determine the stress in the $y$ direction that must be added to restore the $y$-dimension to its original value.
>
> **(b)** What is then the change in volume of the block?

**Key points**

- The block measures 50 / 75 / 100 mm along $x$ / $y$ / $z$; the $y$ direction is the 75 mm one.
- Given $\sigma_x=20\ \text{MPa}$, $\sigma_z=60\ \text{MPa}$ and a temperature rise of 20 °C.
- (a) Find the $\sigma_y$ that restores the $y$ dimension, that is the one that makes $\varepsilon_y=0$.
- (b) With the $\sigma_y$ from (a) already applied, find the **volume change** of the block.

## 2. Answers

| Part | Quantity | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Stress needed to restore the $y$ dimension, $\sigma_y$ | **$\sigma_y=-21.54\ \text{MPa}$** (compressive, $-2.154\times10^{7}\ \text{Pa}$) |
| (b) | Volume change of the block $\Delta V$ | **$+2.90\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}^3$** ($=+290\ \text{mm}^3$) |

**Platform entry**: (a) enter $-21.5\ \text{MPa}$ (or $2.15\times10^{7}\ \text{Pa}$ compressive); (b) **the unit is compulsory** (the platform checks it): $2.90\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}^3$ or $290\ \text{mm}^3$.

## 3. Required knowledge

1. **3D Hooke's law (with thermal strain)**: the strain in each direction is set by all three normal stresses together, plus $\alpha\Delta T$.
2. **Superposition**: mechanical strain and thermal strain add linearly (linear elastic, constant $\alpha$, small deformation).
3. **Writing a constraint as an equation**: the dimension returning to its original value $\Leftrightarrow$ total strain $\varepsilon_y=0$.
4. **Sign convention**: tensile stress is positive; heating ($\Delta T>0$) produces a positive thermal strain $\alpha\Delta T$.
5. **Isotropy**: $\nu$ acts on the stresses in the *other two* directions.
6. **Volumetric strain**: $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y+\varepsilon_z$; the invariant form $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=\dfrac{(\sigma_x+\sigma_y+\sigma_z)(1-2\nu)}{E}+3\alpha\Delta T$ also works.
7. **What is special in part (b)**: $y$ has already been pushed back to its original length, so $\varepsilon_y=0$ and the volume change comes only from $\varepsilon_x$ and $\varepsilon_z$.

## 4. Formulas

**3D Hooke's law (with thermal strain)**

$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{\sigma_x-\nu(\sigma_y+\sigma_z)}{E}+\alpha\Delta T
$$

$$
\varepsilon_y=\frac{\sigma_y-\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)}{E}+\alpha\Delta T
$$

$$
\varepsilon_z=\frac{\sigma_z-\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)}{E}+\alpha\Delta T
$$

**(a) Constraint $\varepsilon_y=0$**

$$
\boxed{\ \sigma_y=\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)-E\alpha\Delta T\ }
$$

**(b) Volume change**

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y+\varepsilon_z
=\frac{(\sigma_x+\sigma_y+\sigma_z)(1-2\nu)}{E}+3\alpha\Delta T
$$

$$
\Delta V=V(\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y+\varepsilon_z),\qquad V=50\times75\times100\ \text{mm}^3
$$

**Symbols**

| Symbol | Meaning | Value here |
|---|---|---|
| $E$ | Young's modulus | $207\ \text{GPa}=207\,000\ \text{MPa}$ |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio | $0.3$ |
| $\alpha$ | Coefficient of linear expansion | $11\times10^{-6}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ |
| $\sigma_x,\sigma_z$ | Tensile stresses in $x$, $z$ | $20\ \text{MPa}$, $60\ \text{MPa}$ |
| $\Delta T$ | Temperature rise | $20\ \text{K}$ |
| $V$ | Volume | $3.75\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^3=375\,000\ \text{mm}^3$ |

## 5. (a) Full working

**Step 1 Uniform units (everything in MPa and K)**

$$
E=207\,000\ \text{MPa},\quad \nu=0.3,\quad \alpha=11\times10^{-6},\quad \Delta T=20
$$

**Step 2 Thermal strain term**

$$
\alpha\Delta T=11\times10^{-6}\times20=2.2\times10^{-4}
$$

$$
E\alpha\Delta T=207\,000\times2.2\times10^{-4}=45.54\ \text{MPa}
$$

**Step 3 Poisson term**

$$
\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)=0.3\times(20+60)=24\ \text{MPa}
$$

**Step 4 Solve from $\varepsilon_y=0$**

$$
0=\frac{\sigma_y-\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)}{E}+\alpha\Delta T
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\sigma_y=\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)-E\alpha\Delta T
$$

$$
\sigma_y=24-45.54=-21.54\ \text{MPa}
$$

$$
\boxed{\sigma_y=-21.54\ \text{MPa}\ (\text{compressive})}
$$

Physical meaning: heating by 20 °C wants to expand the block in the $y$ direction by $2.2\times10^{-4}$, and the tensile stresses in $x$ and $z$ stretch $y$ further through the Poisson effect; pushing it back to the original size therefore needs a compressive stress.

## 6. (b) Full working

**Step 1 The stress state now (the result of (a) is applied)**

$$
\sigma_x=20\ \text{MPa},\qquad \sigma_y=-21.54\ \text{MPa},\qquad \sigma_z=60\ \text{MPa}
$$

and $\varepsilon_y=0$ (the constraint of part (a)).

**Step 2 Compute $\varepsilon_x$**

$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{20-0.3(-21.54+60)}{207\,000}+2.2\times10^{-4}
$$

Bracket first: $-21.54+60=38.46$; $0.3\times38.46=11.538$; $20-11.538=8.462$.

$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{8.462}{207\,000}+2.2\times10^{-4}
=4.088\times10^{-5}+2.2\times10^{-4}
=2.6088\times10^{-4}
$$

**Step 3 Compute $\varepsilon_z$**

$$
\varepsilon_z=\frac{60-0.3(20-21.54)}{207\,000}+2.2\times10^{-4}
$$

$20-21.54=-1.54$; $0.3\times(-1.54)=-0.462$; $60-(-0.462)=60.462$.

$$
\varepsilon_z=\frac{60.462}{207\,000}+2.2\times10^{-4}
=2.9209\times10^{-4}+2.2\times10^{-4}
=5.1209\times10^{-4}
$$

**Step 4 Volumetric strain**

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y+\varepsilon_z
=2.6088\times10^{-4}+0+5.1209\times10^{-4}
=7.7297\times10^{-4}
$$

**Step 5 Multiply by the volume**

$$
V=50\times75\times100\ \text{mm}^3=375\,000\ \text{mm}^3=3.75\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^3
$$

$$
\Delta V=3.75\times10^{-4}\times7.7297\times10^{-4}
=2.899\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}^3
$$

In mm³: $2.899\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}^3\times10^{9}=290\ \text{mm}^3$.

$$
\boxed{\Delta V=+2.90\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}^3\;(+290\ \text{mm}^3)}
$$

**Check with the invariant form** (it should give the same number):

$$
\sigma_x+\sigma_y+\sigma_z=20-21.54+60=58.46\ \text{MPa}
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=\frac{58.46\times(1-2\times0.3)}{207\,000}+3\times2.2\times10^{-4}
=\frac{23.384}{207\,000}+6.6\times10^{-4}
=1.1297\times10^{-4}+6.6\times10^{-4}
=7.7297\times10^{-4}\ \checkmark
$$

## 7. Common mistakes

- **Sign of the thermal strain**: heating expands; restoring $y$ usually needs a compressive stress.
- **Missing a stress**: $\varepsilon_y$ contains $\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)$, so both must be included.
- **Applying $\nu$ to the wrong term**: the coefficient of $\sigma_y$ itself is 1, with no $\nu$.
- **Forgetting $\varepsilon_y=0$ in (b)**: once $\sigma_y$ is applied the $y$ strain is zero, so the volume change is only $\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_z$.
- **Using only $\varepsilon_y$ in (b)**: that is zero, and it is not the volume change.
- **Mixed units**: subtracting a stress in MPa from an $E$ in GPa is out by a factor $10^{3}$; staying in MPa + K is recommended.
- **Volume units**: $375\,000\ \text{mm}^3=3.75\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^3$; do not slip on the $10^{9}$ conversion.
- **Answering the wrong question**: (a) asks for a stress, not a strain; (b) must carry units.
