# Q4.5 Cylinder + hemispherical ends: equal hoop strain and whole-vessel volume change

> **Source**: Lambda Feedback · MECH40005 Stress Analysis 1 (2026–27) · Tutorial sheet 4: Stress and strain · Problem 4.5 (two parts)
> **Keywords**: thin cylinder, hemispherical end, hoop strain, compatibility at the junction, Poisson effect, volumetric strain

## 1. Problem

> The pressure vessel on an air-blast circuit breaker has a thin-walled cylindrical section of mean radius $R$ and thickness $t$.
>
> **(a)** If the vessel has hemispherical ends and the hoop strain of the cylinder and hemispheres at their junction is the same when it is subjected to an internal pressure $P$, calculate the wall thickness, $t'$, of the hemispheres, assuming Poisson's Ratio $\nu=\tfrac13$.
>
> **(b)** If the length of the cylindrical section is $4R$, what is the change in volume of the vessel in terms of $P$, $R$, $t$ and $E$?

**Key points**

- The vessel = a cylinder (radius $R$, thickness $t$) + two hemispherical ends (radius $R$, thickness $t'$).
- (a) At the junction the **hoop strain is the same** in the cylinder and the spheres; with $\nu=1/3$, find $t'$.
- (b) The cylindrical part is $4R$ long; find the volume change of the **whole vessel** (cylinder + two hemispheres) under pressure $P$, in terms of $P,R,t,E$ (using the $t'$ from (a)).

## 2. Answers

| Part | Quantity | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Hemisphere wall thickness $t'$ | **$t'=\dfrac{2}{5}t=0.4t$** |
| (b) | Vessel volume change $\Delta V$ | **$\Delta V=\dfrac{32}{3}\pi\dfrac{PR^{4}}{tE}\approx10.67\pi\dfrac{PR^{4}}{tE}$** |

**Platform entry**: (a) enter $0.4t$ or $2t/5$; (b) follow the hint and write the radius as a fourth power (the platform hints at the R**4 notation), that is $\dfrac{32}{3}\pi\dfrac{PR^{4}}{tE}$.

## 3. Required knowledge

1. **Membrane stress in a thin cylinder** (closed): $\sigma_\theta=\dfrac{PR}{t}$, $\sigma_z=\dfrac{PR}{2t}$.
2. **Membrane stress in a thin sphere**: the two principal stresses are equal, $\sigma_\phi=\sigma_\theta=\dfrac{PR}{2t'}$ (denominator $2t'$).
3. **Plane-stress Hooke's law**: for the cylinder $\varepsilon_\theta=\dfrac{\sigma_\theta-\nu\sigma_z}{E}$; for the sphere the two stresses are equal, so $\varepsilon=\dfrac{\sigma(1-\nu)}{E}$.
4. **Strain compatibility**: the two sides of the junction must deform together ⇒ $\varepsilon_{\theta,\text{cyl}}=\varepsilon_{\theta,\text{sph}}$.
5. **Volumetric strain**: cylinder $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=2\varepsilon_\theta+\varepsilon_z$; sphere $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=3\varepsilon$ (isotropic).
6. **Geometry**: cylinder $V_c=\pi R^{2}(4R)=4\pi R^{3}$; the two hemispheres together $V_s=\dfrac43\pi R^{3}$.
7. **Thin wall, same material, same $R$**: bending at the junction and edge effects are neglected, as is the radius difference caused by the thickness difference; $E$ is the same and cancels.

## 4. Formulas

**Cylinder**

$$
\sigma_\theta^{c}=\frac{PR}{t},\qquad \sigma_z^{c}=\frac{PR}{2t}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{\theta}^{c}=\frac{PR}{tE}\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)
$$

**Sphere**

$$
\sigma_\phi^{s}=\sigma_\theta^{s}=\frac{PR}{2t'},\qquad
\varepsilon_{\theta}^{s}=\frac{PR}{2t'E}(1-\nu)
$$

**(a) Compatibility condition**

$$
\frac{PR}{tE}\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)
=\frac{PR}{2t'E}(1-\nu)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\boxed{\ t'=t\,\frac{1-\nu}{2-\nu}\ }
$$

**(b) Volume change**

$$
\Delta V_{\text{cyl}}=4\pi R^{3}\left(2\varepsilon_\theta^{c}+\varepsilon_z^{c}\right),
\qquad
\Delta V_{\text{sph}}=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}\cdot3\varepsilon^{s}
$$

$$
\boxed{\ \Delta V=\frac{32}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}\ }
$$

**Symbols**

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| $P$ | Internal pressure | symbolic, cancels out |
| $R$ | Mean radius (same for cylinder and spheres) | symbolic |
| $t$ | Cylinder wall thickness | given |
| $t'$ | Hemisphere wall thickness | to be found |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio | $1/3$ |
| $E$ | Young's modulus (same material) | symbolic |

## 5. (a) Full working

**Step 1 Write the hoop stresses on both sides**

Cylinder: $\sigma_\theta^{c}=\dfrac{PR}{t}$, $\sigma_z^{c}=\dfrac{PR}{2t}$.

Sphere: $\sigma_\phi^{s}=\sigma_\theta^{s}=\dfrac{PR}{2t'}$.

**Step 2 Write the hoop strains on both sides**

Cylinder (note the Poisson contribution from the axial stress):

$$
\varepsilon_\theta^{c}=\frac{1}{E}\left(\frac{PR}{t}-\nu\frac{PR}{2t}\right)
=\frac{PR}{tE}\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)
$$

Sphere (the two principal stresses are equal):

$$
\varepsilon_\theta^{s}=\frac{1}{E}\left(\frac{PR}{2t'}-\nu\frac{PR}{2t'}\right)
=\frac{PR}{2t'E}(1-\nu)
$$

**Step 3 Set them equal at the junction**

$$
\frac{PR}{tE}\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)
=\frac{PR}{2t'E}(1-\nu)
$$

$P$, $R$ and $E$ cancel from both sides:

$$
\frac{1}{t}\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)
=\frac{1}{2t'}(1-\nu)
$$

**Step 4 Solve for $t'$**

$$
t'=\frac{t(1-\nu)}{2(1-\nu/2)}=\frac{t(1-\nu)}{2-\nu}
$$

**Step 5 Substitute $\nu=1/3$**

$$
t'=t\cdot\frac{1-\frac13}{2-\frac13}
=t\cdot\frac{2/3}{5/3}
=\frac{2}{5}t=0.4t
$$

$$
\boxed{t'=0.4t}
$$

**Sanity check**: the hoop stress in a sphere is half that in a cylinder, so the sphere can be thinner; $t'<t$ matches intuition ✓.

## 6. (b) Full working (symbolic derivation)

**Step 1 Substitute $\nu=1/3$ and $t'=0.4t$ into both sets of strains**

Cylinder:

$$
\varepsilon_\theta^{c}=\frac{PR}{tE}\left(1-\frac{1}{6}\right)=\frac{5}{6}\frac{PR}{tE}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_z^{c}=\frac{1}{E}\left(\frac{PR}{2t}-\frac13\frac{PR}{t}\right)
=\frac{PR}{tE}\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)=\frac{1}{6}\frac{PR}{tE}
$$

Sphere:

$$
\varepsilon^{s}=\frac{PR}{2(0.4t)E}\left(1-\frac13\right)
=\frac{PR}{0.8tE}\cdot\frac23
=\frac{5}{6}\frac{PR}{tE}
$$

(This equals the hoop strain of the cylinder ✓, which is exactly the compatibility condition of part (a).)

**Step 2 Volume change of the cylinder**

$$
V_c=\pi R^{2}(4R)=4\pi R^{3}
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta V_c}{V_c}=2\varepsilon_\theta^{c}+\varepsilon_z^{c}
=2\cdot\frac56\frac{PR}{tE}+\frac16\frac{PR}{tE}
=\frac{11}{6}\frac{PR}{tE}
$$

$$
\Delta V_c=4\pi R^{3}\cdot\frac{11}{6}\frac{PR}{tE}
=\frac{22}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}
$$

**Step 3 Volume change of the two hemispheres**

Total internal volume of the two hemispheres:

$$
V_s=\frac43\pi R^{3}
$$

The sphere wall is isotropic, so the volumetric strain is $3\varepsilon^{s}$:

$$
\frac{\Delta V_s}{V_s}=3\cdot\frac56\frac{PR}{tE}=\frac52\frac{PR}{tE}
$$

$$
\Delta V_s=\frac43\pi R^{3}\cdot\frac52\frac{PR}{tE}
=\frac{10}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}
$$

**Step 4 Add them**

$$
\Delta V=\Delta V_c+\Delta V_s
=\frac{22}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}+\frac{10}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}
=\frac{32}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta V=\frac{32}{3}\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}\approx10.67\pi\frac{PR^{4}}{tE}}
$$

**Dimensional check**: $\dfrac{P R^{4}}{tE}=\dfrac{(\text{N/m}^2)\,\text{m}^4}{\text{m}\cdot\text{N/m}^2}=\text{m}^3$ ✓.

**Breakdown check**: the cylinder contributes $\tfrac{22}{3}\pi$ and the spheres $\tfrac{10}{3}\pi$, giving $\tfrac{32}{3}\pi$ in total ✓.

## 7. Common mistakes

- **Writing the sphere stress as $\dfrac{PR}{t'}$**: for a sphere it is $\dfrac{PR}{2t'}$.
- **Dropping the axial term in the cylinder strain**: $\varepsilon_\theta=\dfrac{\sigma_\theta-\nu\sigma_z}{E}$.
- **Subtracting $\nu\sigma$ only once for the sphere**: the two principal stresses are equal, so it is $\sigma(1-\nu)/E$.
- **Forgetting that there are two hemispheres**: their combined volume is $\tfrac43\pi R^3$, not $\tfrac23\pi R^3$ - the two hemispheres together make exactly one full sphere.
- **Using $t$ instead of $t'$ for the spheres**: part (b) must use $t'=0.4t$ from part (a) to end up with an expression in $t$ alone.
- **Missing the factor 2 in the volumetric strain**: for the cylinder it is $2\varepsilon_\theta+\varepsilon_z$; for the sphere it is $3\varepsilon$.
- **Bending at the junction**: membrane theory neglects edge effects, and the question assumes this.
