# Q4.1 Tension induced by tightening a bolt

> **Source**: Lambda Feedback · MECH40005 Stress Analysis 1 (2026–27) · Tutorial sheet 4: Stress and strain · Problem 4.1 (single part)
> **Keywords**: axial stiffness, compatibility, thread pitch, rigid clamped parts

## 1. Problem

> A mild steel ($E=207\ \text{GPa}$) bolt has a minimum diameter of 8 mm and there is one thread per mm. The bolt is one of several to be used to fasten a generator to a base and the mating parts can be considered to be rigid. When the parts are just brought together as shown below, the distance between the head of the bolt and the nut is 0.25 m.
>
> Neglecting end effects in the bolts, estimate the tension which will be induced in the bolt if the nut is then tightened by one sixth of a turn.

**Key points**: the clamped parts are treated as rigid (they do not compress), so a $1/6$ turn of the nut = an axial advance of $1/6$ of the thread pitch = $1/6$ mm. That displacement can only be absorbed by stretching the bolt. Find the bolt tension $F$.

## 2. Answer at a glance

| Quantity | Answer | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bolt tension $F$ | **$F \approx 6.94\ \text{kN}$** ($6.94\times10^{3}\ \text{N}$) | Uses minimum diameter 8 mm, $L=0.25\ \text{m}$ |
| Also obtained: bolt stress $\sigma$ | $138\ \text{MPa}$ | Useful as a self-check ($\sigma=Ep/(6L)$, independent of diameter) |

**Platform entry**: give the unit as N or kN; if the platform wants N, enter $6.94\times10^{3}$; 3 significant figures is enough.

## 3. Required knowledge

1. **Axial deformation and Hooke's law**: $\delta=\dfrac{FL}{EA}$, equivalent to a spring stiffness $k=\dfrac{EA}{L}$.
2. **Load path in a bolted joint**: tightening puts the bolt in tension and the clamped parts in compression; total deformation = bolt extension + compression of the parts.
3. **What "rigid" means**: clamped parts rigid $\Rightarrow$ zero compression, so the whole displacement budget is carried by bolt extension.
4. **Thread pitch (pitch / lead)**: one full turn of the nut advances it one pitch $p$ along the axis; $1/6$ of a turn advances it $p/6$.
5. **Compatibility**: the plate thickness does not change $\Rightarrow$ the extra distance the nut travels along the thread can only be made up by stretching the bolt, that is $\delta=p/6$.
6. **Stress**: $\sigma=F/A$, with the area taken on the **minimum diameter** (thread root).
7. **Small deformation, linear elastic**: $E$ is constant; bending of the bolt, local thread-tooth deformation and end effects are neglected.

## 4. Formulas

$$
k=\frac{EA}{L},\qquad \delta=\frac{FL}{EA}
$$

$$
A=\frac{\pi d^{2}}{4}\qquad(d=8\ \text{mm})
$$

**Compatibility condition (rigid clamped parts)**

$$
\delta=\frac{p}{6}\qquad(p=1\ \text{mm})
$$

**Required tension**

$$
F=k\delta=\frac{EA}{L}\cdot\frac{p}{6}=\frac{E\,(\pi d^{2}/4)\,p}{6L}
$$

**Stress obtained along the way (independent of diameter)**

$$
\sigma=\frac{F}{A}=\frac{E\delta}{L}=\frac{Ep}{6L}
$$

**Symbols**

| Symbol | Meaning | Value here |
|---|---|---|
| $E$ | Young's modulus | $207\ \text{GPa}=207\times10^{9}\ \text{Pa}$ |
| $d$ | Minimum bolt diameter | $8\ \text{mm}=0.008\ \text{m}$ |
| $L$ | Clamped length, head to nut | $0.25\ \text{m}$ |
| $p$ | Thread pitch (1 thread/mm) | $1\ \text{mm}=10^{-3}\ \text{m}$ |
| $A$ | Minimum cross-sectional area | $\pi(0.008)^2/4=5.027\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2$ |

## 5. Full worked solution

**Step 1 Uniform units**

$$
E=207\times10^{9}\ \text{Pa},\quad d=0.008\ \text{m},\quad L=0.25\ \text{m},\quad p=1\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}
$$

**Step 2 Minimum cross-sectional area**

$$
A=\frac{\pi d^{2}}{4}=\frac{\pi(0.008)^{2}}{4}
=5.0265\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2\;(=50.27\ \text{mm}^2)
$$

**Step 3 Nut advance (= the extension the bolt must take up)**

$$
\Delta=\frac{p}{6}=\frac{1\ \text{mm}}{6}=0.16667\ \text{mm}=1.6667\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}
$$

Because the clamped parts are rigid and do not compress, the bolt extension equals this advance:

$$
\delta=\Delta=1.6667\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}
$$

**Step 4 Tension**

$$
F=\frac{EA\delta}{L}
=\frac{207\times10^{9}\times5.0265\times10^{-5}\times1.6667\times10^{-4}}{0.25}
$$

Numerator first: $207\times10^{9}\times5.0265\times10^{-5}=1.0405\times10^{7}\ \text{N}$ (this is $EA$), then multiply by $\delta$: $1.0405\times10^{7}\times1.6667\times10^{-4}=1734.2\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$.

$$
F=\frac{1734.2}{0.25}=6.937\times10^{3}\ \text{N}
$$

$$
\boxed{F\approx6.94\ \text{kN}}
$$

**Step 5 Self-check (stress)**

$$
\sigma=\frac{F}{A}=\frac{6.937\times10^{3}}{5.0265\times10^{-5}}
=1.38\times10^{8}\ \text{Pa}=138\ \text{MPa}
$$

The diameter-independent form gives the same result: $\sigma=\dfrac{Ep}{6L}=\dfrac{207\times10^{9}\times10^{-3}}{6\times0.25}=138\ \text{MPa}$ ✓. 138 MPa is far below the yield strength of mild steel (about 250–300 MPa), so the linear elastic assumption holds.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Wrong diameter**: 8 mm is the **minimum diameter** (thread root), not the nominal diameter; do not drop the $\pi/4$ in the area.
- **Pitch and turns**: 1 thread/mm means $p=1\ \text{mm}$; $1/6$ of a turn $\Rightarrow 1/6\ \text{mm}$, not $6\ \text{mm}$.
- **Treating the clamped parts as elastic**: the question says rigid, so you cannot share the load as "bolt + plate in series"; doing so underestimates the tension.
- **Mixed units**: multiplying GPa directly by mm is the most common slip; converting everything to Pa + m is safest.
- **Wrong length**: $L$ is the 0.25 m clamped length from head to nut, not the overall bolt length or the threaded length.
- **End effects**: the question allows neglecting the local flexibility of head and nut; do not add your own correction factor.
