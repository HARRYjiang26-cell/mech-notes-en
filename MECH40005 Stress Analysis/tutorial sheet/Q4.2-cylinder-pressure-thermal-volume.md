# Q4.2 Thin-walled cylinder: volume change from internal pressure + heating

> **Source**: Lambda Feedback · MECH40005 Stress Analysis 1 (2026–27) · Tutorial sheet 4: Stress and strain · Problem 4.2 (two parts)
> **Keywords**: thin-walled vessel, hoop & axial stress, plane stress, volumetric strain, thermal expansion

## 1. Problem

> A closed-ended cylindrical thin-walled pressure vessel is made of aluminium ($E=69\ \text{GPa}$, $\nu=0.3$, $\alpha=23\times10^{-6}\ \text{K}^{-1}$) and is characterised by the following dimensions: mean radius $R_m=2\ \text{m}$, length $L=2\ \text{m}$, and thickness $t=120\ \text{mm}$.
>
> **(a)** Calculate the total change in volume due to a 5 MPa internal pressure and a 25 °C increase in temperature.
>
> **(b)** What would be the change in volume of a spherical thin-walled tank which had the same mean radius and thickness as the cylindrical pressure vessel above?

**Key points**

- Closed-ended thin-walled cylinder: the wall carries two principal stresses, hoop $\sigma_\theta$ and axial $\sigma_z$, with radial $\sigma_r\approx0$ (plane stress).
- (a) The cylinder is loaded by **5 MPa internal pressure** and by a **25 °C temperature rise** at the same time; find the total change of the **internal volume** $\Delta V$.
- (b) Put a **spherical tank** of the same radius and the same wall thickness under the same conditions (5 MPa, +25 °C) and find $\Delta V$.

## 2. Answers

| Part | Quantity | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Cylinder volume change $\Delta V_{\text{cylinder}}$ | **$0.1010\ \text{m}^3$** (≈101 L) |
| (b) | Spherical tank volume change $\Delta V_{\text{sphere}}$ | **$0.1003\ \text{m}^3$** (≈100 L) |

**Platform entry**: use $\text{m}^3$ (or L, stating which); 3–4 significant figures is enough. If the platform counts in L, the values are 101.0 L and 100.3 L.

## 3. Required knowledge

1. **Membrane stress in a thin-walled cylinder** (closed ends): $\sigma_\theta=\dfrac{pR}{t}$ (hoop, from radial equilibrium of a half cylinder), $\sigma_z=\dfrac{pR}{2t}$ (axial, from pressure equilibrium on the end caps).
2. **Membrane stress in a thin-walled sphere**: spherically symmetric, so the two principal stresses are equal, $\sigma_\phi=\sigma_\theta=\dfrac{pR}{2t}$ (the denominator is $2t$, not $t$).
3. **Plane-stress Hooke's law + thermal strain**: $\varepsilon=\dfrac{\sigma-\nu\sigma'}{E}+\alpha\Delta T$.
4. **Volumetric strain**: cylinder $V=\pi R^{2}L$, $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=2\varepsilon_\theta+\varepsilon_z$; sphere $V=\dfrac43\pi R^{3}$, $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=3\varepsilon$ (isotropic, the same strain in all three directions).
5. **Thermal expansion**: $\alpha\Delta T$ in each direction, so the **volumetric** thermal strain is $3\alpha\Delta T$.
6. **Superposition**: linear elastic, small deformation ⇒ the pressure term and the temperature term add directly.
7. **Thin-wall assumption**: $R/t\gg1$; here $R/t\approx16.7$, which is rather thick, but the question treats it as thin-walled, so the thick-walled Lamé solution is not used.

## 4. Formulas

**Cylinder**

$$
\sigma_\theta=\frac{pR}{t},\qquad \sigma_z=\frac{pR}{2t},\qquad V=\pi R^{2}L
$$

$$
\varepsilon_\theta=\frac{\sigma_\theta-\nu\sigma_z}{E}+\alpha\Delta T,\qquad
\varepsilon_z=\frac{\sigma_z-\nu\sigma_\theta}{E}+\alpha\Delta T
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=2\varepsilon_\theta+\varepsilon_z
$$

**Sphere**

$$
\sigma=\frac{pR}{2t},\qquad V=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}
$$

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{\sigma(1-\nu)}{E}+\alpha\Delta T,\qquad
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=3\varepsilon
$$

**Combined pressure term (cylinder, convenient for checking)**

$$
2\varepsilon_\theta^{p}+\varepsilon_z^{p}
=\frac{pR}{tE}\left(\frac52-2\nu\right)
$$

**Symbols**

| Symbol | Meaning | Value here |
|---|---|---|
| $p$ | Internal pressure | $5\ \text{MPa}=5\times10^{6}\ \text{Pa}$ |
| $R$ | Mean radius | $2\ \text{m}$ |
| $L$ | Cylinder length | $2\ \text{m}$ |
| $t$ | Wall thickness | $0.12\ \text{m}$ |
| $E$ | Young's modulus of aluminium | $69\times10^{9}\ \text{Pa}$ |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio | $0.3$ |
| $\alpha$ | Coefficient of linear expansion | $23\times10^{-6}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ |
| $\Delta T$ | Temperature rise | $25\ \text{K}$ |

## 5. (a) Full working

**Step 1 Cylinder volume**

$$
V=\pi R^{2}L=\pi(2)^{2}(2)=8\pi=25.133\ \text{m}^3
$$

**Step 2 The two membrane stresses**

$$
\sigma_\theta=\frac{pR}{t}=\frac{5\times10^{6}\times2}{0.12}=83.333\ \text{MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_z=\frac{pR}{2t}=\frac{5\times10^{6}\times2}{2\times0.12}=41.667\ \text{MPa}
$$

**Step 3 The two strains (pressure part)**

$$
\varepsilon_\theta^{p}=\frac{83.333-0.3\times41.667}{69\times10^{3}}
=\frac{83.333-12.500}{69000}
=1.0266\times10^{-3}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_z^{p}=\frac{41.667-0.3\times83.333}{69000}
=\frac{41.667-25.000}{69000}
=2.4155\times10^{-4}
$$

(Here the stresses are in MPa and $E$ is in MPa: $69\ \text{GPa}=69\,000\ \text{MPa}$.)

**Step 4 Volume change from pressure**

$$
\frac{\Delta V_p}{V}=2\varepsilon_\theta^{p}+\varepsilon_z^{p}
=2(1.0266\times10^{-3})+2.4155\times10^{-4}
=2.2947\times10^{-3}
$$

$$
\Delta V_p=25.133\times2.2947\times10^{-3}=5.767\times10^{-2}\ \text{m}^3
$$

**Step 5 Volume change from temperature**

$$
3\alpha\Delta T=3\times23\times10^{-6}\times25=1.725\times10^{-3}
$$

$$
\Delta V_T=25.133\times1.725\times10^{-3}=4.335\times10^{-2}\ \text{m}^3
$$

**Step 6 Add them**

$$
\Delta V=\Delta V_p+\Delta V_T
=5.767\times10^{-2}+4.335\times10^{-2}
=1.010\times10^{-1}\ \text{m}^3
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta V_{\text{cylinder}}\approx0.101\ \text{m}^3\ (\approx101\ \text{L})}
$$

Self-check: $V\approx25\ \text{m}^3$ and the volumetric strain is about $4.0\times10^{-3}$, so $\Delta V$ should be of order $0.1\ \text{m}^3$ ✓.

## 6. (b) Full working (sphere)

**Step 1 Sphere volume**

$$
V_s=\frac43\pi R^{3}=\frac43\pi(2)^{3}=\frac{32\pi}{3}=33.510\ \text{m}^3
$$

**Step 2 Membrane stress in the sphere**

$$
\sigma=\frac{pR}{2t}=\frac{5\times10^{6}\times2}{2\times0.12}=41.667\ \text{MPa}
$$

(Note: both principal stresses in the sphere equal this value.)

**Step 3 Sphere strain (plane stress + thermal strain)**

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{\sigma(1-\nu)}{E}=\frac{41.667\times(1-0.3)}{69\times10^{3}}
=\frac{29.167}{69000}=4.2271\times10^{-4}
$$

**Step 4 Volume change from pressure (isotropic ⇒ volumetric strain = $3\varepsilon$)**

$$
\frac{\Delta V_p}{V_s}=3\varepsilon=3\times4.2271\times10^{-4}=1.2681\times10^{-3}
$$

$$
\Delta V_p=33.510\times1.2681\times10^{-3}=4.249\times10^{-2}\ \text{m}^3
$$

**Step 5 Volume change from temperature**

$$
\Delta V_T=33.510\times1.725\times10^{-3}=5.781\times10^{-2}\ \text{m}^3
$$

**Step 6 Add them**

$$
\Delta V_s=4.249\times10^{-2}+5.781\times10^{-2}
=1.003\times10^{-1}\ \text{m}^3
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta V_{\text{sphere}}\approx0.100\ \text{m}^3\ (\approx100\ \text{L})}
$$

**Comparison**: the stress in the sphere is only half the hoop stress in the cylinder, so its pressure term is smaller; but the sphere encloses a larger volume ($33.5$ vs $25.1\ \text{m}^3$), so its thermal term is larger. The two effects cancel and the answers come out very close (0.101 vs 0.100 m³). That is a sound result, not a coincidence.

## 7. Common mistakes

- **Closed or open**: only a closed cylinder has $\sigma_z=\dfrac{pR}{2t}$; an open container has $\sigma_z=0$.
- **Missing the factor 2 in the volumetric strain**: for a cylinder it is $2\varepsilon_\theta+\varepsilon_z$.
- **Writing the thermal term as $\alpha\Delta T$**: for a volume it must be $3\alpha\Delta T$.
- **Writing the sphere stress as $\dfrac{pR}{t}$**: for a sphere it is $\dfrac{pR}{2t}$; dropping the 2 doubles the strain.
- **Dropping the Poisson term in the axial strain**: $\varepsilon_z\ne\sigma_z/E$.
- **Units**: $t=120\ \text{mm}=0.12\ \text{m}$; do not mix MPa with Pa or GPa with MPa.
- **Treating $\Delta V$ as the wall material volume**: the question asks for the **internal volume** change.
- **(b) Loading**: compute it for the same conditions as (a) (5 MPa, +25 °C); if the platform used other conditions the numbers would differ.
