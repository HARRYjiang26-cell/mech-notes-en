# Q4.4 Laterally constrained cast-iron block: apparent modulus (two constraint cases)

> **Source**: Lambda Feedback · MECH40005 Stress Analysis 1 (2026–27) · Tutorial sheet 4: Stress and strain · Problem 4.4* (two parts)
> **Keywords**: lateral constraint, plane-strain-like, apparent Young's modulus, lateral pressure

## 1. Problem

> A block of cast iron ($E=115\ \text{GPa}$, $\nu=0.25$) is 150 mm long and 25 mm square. It is compressed by a force of 90 kN applied parallel to its 150 mm direction while all lateral strain is prevented by the application of uniform lateral pressure to all four long faces.
>
> **(a)** Find the change in length, the apparent modulus of elasticity and the lateral pressure.
>
> **(b)** If the lateral pressure were applied to only two opposing faces, thus preventing lateral strain in one direction only, the other two faces being unrestrained, what would then be the change in length, the apparent modulus of elasticity and the lateral pressure?

**Key points**

- Cast-iron block: length $L=150\ \text{mm}$ (the $y$ direction), cross-section $25\times25\ \text{mm}^2$; compressed along $y$ by 90 kN.
- (a) Lateral pressure on **all four** long faces, so both lateral strains are prevented: $\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_z=0$.
- (b) Lateral pressure on **one pair** of long faces only: only $\varepsilon_x=0$ is prevented; the other pair is free, so $\sigma_z=0$ and $\varepsilon_z$ is free.
- Both parts ask for three things: $\Delta L_y$, the apparent modulus $E_{\text{app}}=\sigma_y/\varepsilon_y$, and the lateral pressure applied $|\sigma_x|$.

## 2. Answers

| Part | Quantity | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) four-face constraint | $\Delta L_y$ | **$-0.157\ \text{mm}$** (shortening of $0.1565\ \text{mm}$) |
| | $E_{\text{app}}$ | **$138\ \text{GPa}$** ($=1.2E$) |
| | Lateral pressure $p_x=|\sigma_x|$ | **$48\ \text{MPa}$** |
| (b) two-face constraint | $\Delta L_y$ | **$-0.176\ \text{mm}$** (shortening of $0.1761\ \text{mm}$) |
| | $E_{\text{app}}$ | **$122.7\ \text{GPa}$** ($=E/(1-\nu^2)$) |
| | Lateral pressure $p_x=|\sigma_x|$ | **$36\ \text{MPa}$** |

**Platform entry**: length in mm (or in m: $-1.57\times10^{-4}$ m / $-1.76\times10^{-4}$ m); modulus in GPa; lateral pressure in MPa. The negative sign means compression; if the platform asks for magnitude, give the positive value.

## 3. Required knowledge

1. **Uniaxial compressive stress**: $\sigma_y=-F/A$, $A=25\times25\ \text{mm}^2$; compression is negative.
2. **3D Hooke's law**: the three normal stresses are coupled through $\nu$.
3. **Lateral constraint**: applying lateral pressure makes one transverse strain zero, so the constraint is written $\varepsilon=0$.
4. **Symmetry**: (a) pressure on all four faces ⇒ $\sigma_x=\sigma_z$; (b) only one pair ⇒ $\sigma_z=0$, $\sigma_x\ne0$.
5. **Apparent modulus**: fold the constraint effect into the stiffness, $E_{\text{app}}=\sigma_y/\varepsilon_y$.
6. **The difference between the two constraints**:
   - biaxial constraint ($\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_z=0$): $E_{\text{app}}=\dfrac{E(1-\nu)}{1-\nu-2\nu^2}$;
   - uniaxial constraint ($\varepsilon_x=0,\ \sigma_z=0$): $E_{\text{app}}=\dfrac{E}{1-\nu^2}$.
   Both are larger than $E$.
7. **Physical picture of the Poisson effect**: under compression the material wants to bulge sideways, and the lateral pressure pushes it back; the lateral pressure then "helps" the axial compression through the Poisson effect in return.

## 4. Formulas

**Axial stress**

$$
\sigma_y=-\frac{F}{A},\qquad A=25\times25\ \text{mm}^2=625\ \text{mm}^2
$$

**(a) Four-face constraint ($\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_z=0$)**

$$
\sigma_x=\sigma_z=\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}\sigma_y
$$

$$
\varepsilon_y=\frac{\sigma_y}{E}\left(1-\frac{2\nu^{2}}{1-\nu}\right)
=\frac{\sigma_y}{E}\cdot\frac{1-\nu-2\nu^{2}}{1-\nu}
$$

$$
E_{\text{app}}^{(a)}=\frac{\sigma_y}{\varepsilon_y}
=E\,\frac{1-\nu}{1-\nu-2\nu^{2}}
$$

**(b) Two-face constraint ($\varepsilon_x=0,\ \sigma_z=0$)**

$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{\sigma_x-\nu(\sigma_y+0)}{E}=0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\sigma_x=\nu\sigma_y
$$

$$
\varepsilon_y=\frac{\sigma_y-\nu(\sigma_x+0)}{E}
=\frac{\sigma_y(1-\nu^{2})}{E}
$$

$$
E_{\text{app}}^{(b)}=\frac{E}{1-\nu^{2}}
$$

**Change in length (common to both parts)**

$$
\Delta L_y=\varepsilon_y L
$$

**Simplified for $\nu=0.25$ in this problem**

$$
(a):\ \sigma_x=\frac{\sigma_y}{3},\quad E_{\text{app}}=1.2E
\qquad
(b):\ \sigma_x=\frac{\sigma_y}{4},\quad E_{\text{app}}=\frac{16}{15}E\approx1.067E
$$

**Symbols**

| Symbol | Meaning | Value here |
|---|---|---|
| $F$ | Axial compressive force | $90\ \text{kN}=9\times10^{4}\ \text{N}$ |
| $A$ | Cross-sectional area | $625\ \text{mm}^2=6.25\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2$ |
| $L$ | Length | $150\ \text{mm}$ |
| $E$ | Young's modulus of cast iron | $115\ \text{GPa}=115\,000\ \text{MPa}$ |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio | $0.25$ |

## 5. (a) Full working

**Step 1 Axial compressive stress**

$$
\sigma_y=-\frac{F}{A}=-\frac{90\times10^{3}\ \text{N}}{625\times10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2}
=-1.44\times10^{8}\ \text{Pa}=-144\ \text{MPa}
$$

**Step 2 Set $\sigma_x=\sigma_z$ by symmetry and solve $\varepsilon_x=0$ for the lateral pressure**

$$
0=\frac{\sigma_x-\nu(\sigma_y+\sigma_x)}{E}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\sigma_x(1-\nu)=\nu\sigma_y
$$

$$
\sigma_x=\frac{0.25}{0.75}\times(-144)=-48\ \text{MPa}
$$

So the lateral pressure has magnitude $|\sigma_x|=48\ \text{MPa}$.

**Step 3 Axial strain**

$$
\varepsilon_y=\frac{\sigma_y-2\nu\sigma_x}{E}
=\frac{-144-2(0.25)(-48)}{115\,000}
=\frac{-144+24}{115\,000}
=-1.0435\times10^{-3}
$$

**Step 4 Change in length**

$$
\Delta L_y=\varepsilon_y L=-1.0435\times10^{-3}\times150\ \text{mm}=-0.1565\ \text{mm}
$$

**Step 5 Apparent modulus**

$$
E_{\text{app}}=\frac{|\sigma_y|}{|\varepsilon_y|}
=\frac{144}{1.0435\times10^{-3}}=1.380\times10^{5}\ \text{MPa}=138\ \text{GPa}
$$

Or straight from the formula: $E\dfrac{1-\nu}{1-\nu-2\nu^2}=115\times\dfrac{0.75}{0.75-0.125}=115\times1.2=138\ \text{GPa}$ ✓

$$
\boxed{\Delta L_y=-0.157\ \text{mm};\quad E_{\text{app}}=138\ \text{GPa};\quad p_x=48\ \text{MPa}}
$$

## 6. (b) Full working

**Step 1 The new constraint conditions**

Pressure is applied to one pair of side faces only (take them as the $x$ faces): $\varepsilon_x=0$; the other pair (the $z$ faces) is free: $\sigma_z=0$.

**Step 2 Solve $\varepsilon_x=0$ for the lateral pressure**

$$
0=\frac{\sigma_x-\nu(\sigma_y+0)}{E}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\sigma_x=\nu\sigma_y=0.25\times(-144)=-36\ \text{MPa}
$$

Lateral pressure magnitude $36\ \text{MPa}$.

**Step 3 Axial strain**

$$
\varepsilon_y=\frac{\sigma_y-\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)}{E}
=\frac{-144-0.25(-36+0)}{115\,000}
=\frac{-144+9}{115\,000}
=-1.1739\times10^{-3}
$$

**Step 4 Change in length**

$$
\Delta L_y=\varepsilon_y L=-1.1739\times10^{-3}\times150\ \text{mm}=-0.1761\ \text{mm}
$$

**Step 5 Apparent modulus**

$$
E_{\text{app}}=\frac{|\sigma_y|}{|\varepsilon_y|}
=\frac{144}{1.1739\times10^{-3}}=1.2267\times10^{5}\ \text{MPa}=122.7\ \text{GPa}
$$

Check: $E_{\text{app}}=\dfrac{E}{1-\nu^2}=\dfrac{115}{1-0.0625}=\dfrac{115}{0.9375}=122.7\ \text{GPa}$ ✓

$$
\boxed{\Delta L_y=-0.176\ \text{mm};\quad E_{\text{app}}=122.7\ \text{GPa};\quad p_x=36\ \text{MPa}}
$$

**Comparison**: (b) constrains only one direction, so the constraint is weaker ⇒ smaller lateral pressure (36 vs 48 MPa), lower apparent modulus (122.7 vs 138 GPa) and more axial compression (0.176 vs 0.157 mm). All three trends agree ✓.

## 7. Common mistakes

- **Taking the apparent modulus to be $E$**: under both constraints $E_{\text{app}}$ is larger than $E$: (a) $1.2E$, (b) $1.067E$.
- **Dropping the second lateral stress**: in (a) $\varepsilon_y$ contains $2\nu\sigma_x$; in (b) $\sigma_z=0$, so only one term appears.
- **Signs**: $F$ is compressive, so $\sigma_y<0$; the lateral pressure is also compressive (negative), and the question's word "pressure" asks for the magnitude.
- **Getting the constrained direction wrong**: it is the lateral strain that is prevented, not the axial one; the block is still free to shorten axially.
- **Wrong area**: a 25 mm square is $625\ \text{mm}^2$; the 150 mm is the length and does not enter the area.
- **Units**: 90 kN $=9\times10^{4}$ N; do not mix MPa with Pa or mm with m.
- **Reusing the (a) formulas in (b)**: the constraint has changed, so both $E_{\text{app}}$ and the lateral pressure must be recomputed.
